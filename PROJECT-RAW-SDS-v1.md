## CHAPTER 1: MISSION, PHILOSOPHY & VALUES

### 1.1 Mission Statement

**To Push Growth.**

Project.RAW exists to create a trusted circle of individuals committed to personal growth, accountability, meaningful relationships, and honest conversation.

This sentence is not a slogan. It is the organizational filter through which every decision, every nomination, every vote, and every governance action must pass.

### 1.2 What Project.RAW Is Not

Project.RAW is not a social club.  
Project.RAW is not a networking group.  
Project.RAW is not a podcast.  
Project.RAW is not a mastermind group transacting business.  
Project.RAW is not a support group seeking comfort.

Project.RAW is a circle where individuals intentionally gather to sharpen one another through challenge, support, reflection, and action.

The distinction matters. Social clubs optimize for enjoyment. Networking groups optimize for transactions. Project.RAW optimizes for growth. These are not the same thing. Members who confuse them will struggle to understand why the standards exist.

### 1.3 The Three Pillars

#### Pillar One: Pursue Growth

We believe growth is a lifelong responsibility — not a phase, not a phase of life, not something that happens in your twenties. We reject stagnation actively, not passively. We do not simply avoid declining — we seek improvement.

Growth is intentional. It does not happen by accident. It requires direction, effort, reflection, and adjustment.

A member who is not actively pursuing growth in at least one significant area of their life is not fulfilling the first pillar.

#### Pillar Two: Seek Accountability

We invite honest feedback. We accept responsibility for our actions. We hold ourselves to a higher standard than the world requires.

Accountability is not punishment. It is not shame. It is not public humiliation. Accountability is the practice of naming what is true, committing to what must change, and following through.

A member who deflects, minimizes, or refuses honest feedback is not fulfilling the second pillar.

#### Pillar Three: Embrace Discomfort

We recognize that meaningful growth rarely occurs within comfort. We welcome difficult conversations, constructive criticism, challenging experiences, and opportunities that stretch us beyond our current limitations.

Discomfort is often the price of progress. A member who consistently avoids difficult conversations or retreats when challenged is not fulfilling the third pillar.

### 1.4 Guiding Principle

**Iron Sharpens Iron.**

Growth occurs when individuals challenge, support, encourage, and refine one another through authentic relationships and honest conversation. One piece of iron does not sharpen itself. The circle is the mechanism.

### 1.5 Project.RAW Philosophy

> Growth is the destination.  
> Accountability is the mechanism.  
> Discomfort is the price.

### 1.6 The Organizational Filter

All decisions within Project.RAW — governance decisions, nomination decisions, programming decisions, operational decisions — are evaluated against a single question:

**Does this push growth?**

If the answer is no, it likely does not belong within Project.RAW. If the answer is unclear, the decision requires deliberation. This filter is not a suggestion. It is the operating system of the circle.

### 1.7 The RAW Code

Members of Project.RAW commit to the following code of conduct, which governs behavior within sessions, within the community, and in the spirit of membership:

1. We tell the truth.
2. We pursue growth.
3. We remain teachable.
4. We contribute.
5. We challenge respectfully.
6. We protect trust.
7. We leave people better than we found them.

### 1.8 The Project.RAW Standard

Every member commits to:

- Pursuing Growth
- Seeking Accountability
- Embracing Discomfort
- Speaking Truthfully
- Remaining Teachable
- Contributing To Others
- Protecting Trust

## CHAPTER 2: ORGANIZATIONAL STRUCTURE

### 2.1 Overview

Project.RAW operates through a combination of human leadership, procedural governance, and AI-facilitated administration. The purpose of governance is not control. The purpose of governance is to preserve culture, values, trust, and mission alignment.

### 2.2 Organizational Chart

```
PROJECT.RAW
│
├── MARKUS (Keeper of the Circle)
│   └── AI Administrative Overseer
│   └── Protocol Administrator
│   └── Record Keeper
│   └── No voting authority
│
├── STEWARDS (Guardians of Culture)
│   └── Defined; not yet activated
│   └── Activation trigger: 10 full Members + 12 months of operation
│   └── Authority: Governance review, culture protection, conflict resolution
│
├── MEMBERS (The Circle)
│   └── Full voting authority
│   └── Nomination rights
│   └── Sponsorship rights
│   └── Governance participation
│
├── PLEDGES (Integration Phase)
│   └── Participation rights
│   └── No voting, nominating, or governance authority
│
└── NOMINEES (Evaluation Phase)
    └── No rights until approved
    └── Onboarding obligations only
```

### 2.3 Founding and Charter Member Recognition

All individuals who are active participants in Project.RAW at the time this document is formally ratified by the circle are recognized as **Charter Members**.

Charter Members receive full Member rights as defined in Chapter 3. No pledge period is required retroactively. Charter Member status is noted in the permanent governance record maintained by Markus.

Charter Members are not exempt from ongoing Member responsibilities, the RAW Code, or any future governance processes.

### 2.4 The Founder's Role

The founder of Project.RAW participates in the circle as a Member, subject to all Member rights and responsibilities. The founder does not hold special veto authority, does not have permanent override capability, and is not exempt from any governance process.

The founder's role in the early stages of the organization is to act as primary cultural steward until formal Stewards are appointed. This responsibility does not grant extra votes. It grants extra obligation.

### 2.5 Governance Philosophy

Project.RAW governs itself through documented process, not personal authority. Markus exists to protect the process. Members exist to exercise it. Stewards exist to preserve it.

No individual — including the founder — may circumvent documented process without the documented consent of the full circle.

### 2.6 Steward Activation Trigger

Stewards are defined in this document but are not active until the following conditions are both met:

1. The circle has reached **ten (10) full Members** (excluding nominees and pledges).
2. The circle has been formally operating under this governance framework for at least **twelve (12) months**.

When both conditions are met, Markus notifies the circle that Steward activation is available. The circle then initiates the Steward selection process as defined in Chapter 10.

Until Stewards are activated, all Steward-level governance responsibilities are handled by full Member deliberation facilitated by Markus.

### 2.7 Meeting Cadence

Project.RAW sessions occur at a minimum frequency of **once per month**. The recommended cadence is **bi-weekly (every two weeks)**.

Session format, location, duration, and agenda are determined by the active Members. Markus tracks attendance and flags members who fall below the participation threshold defined in Chapter 3.

Sessions are private. Non-members may not attend sessions without explicit, unanimous, advance approval from active Members, and such attendance does not confer any membership status.

## CHAPTER 3: MEMBERSHIP HIERARCHY & RIGHTS

### 3.1 The Four Classifications

Project.RAW membership exists in four classifications. Movement between classifications follows defined protocols and cannot be accelerated or bypassed.

#### 3.1.1 Nominee

A nominee is an individual under active consideration for entry into Project.RAW.

**Rights:** None.  
**Obligations:** Complete onboarding process as directed by Markus.  
**Duration:** From nomination activation through voting outcome.

#### 3.1.2 Pledge

A pledge is an approved nominee currently serving the integration period.

**Rights:**
- Attend all sessions
- Participate in discussions
- Access shared resources
- Join activities
- Build relationships with members
- Contribute ideas

**Restrictions:**
- May not vote on any matter
- May not nominate candidates
- May not sponsor nominees
- May not serve on committees or review councils
- May not hold stewardship positions
- May not propose governance amendments

**Obligations:**
- Attend consistently (see participation threshold below)
- Participate authentically
- Maintain confidentiality
- Complete all assigned check-ins and self-assessments
- Remain coachable
- Cooperate with sponsor feedback process

**Duration:** Six (6) months from pledge admission date, subject to extension or early release as defined in Chapter 8.

#### 3.1.3 Member

A member is a pledge who has completed the integration period and received membership approval.

**Rights:**
- Vote on all membership matters
- Nominate candidates
- Sponsor nominees
- Participate in governance
- Serve on committees (once defined)
- Become eligible for Steward selection (once Stewards are activated)
- Propose governance amendments

**Obligations:**
- Protect the culture
- Mentor pledges (especially those they have sponsored)
- Uphold confidentiality
- Participate honestly and consistently
- Vote responsibly and within established windows
- Lead by example
- Defend the mission

**Active Member Definition:** A member is considered "active" for governance purposes if they have attended at least one session in the preceding sixty (60) calendar days OR have formally notified Markus of a leave of absence. Only active members count toward quorum and voting requirements.

**Leave of Absence:** A member may request a leave of absence from Markus for personal circumstances. Leave may not exceed ninety (90) consecutive days without circle review. Members on leave are not counted toward quorum but retain all rights upon return.

**Participation Threshold:** A member who misses more than fifty percent (50%) of sessions in any rolling ninety (90) day period without an approved leave of absence is flagged by Markus as inactive and subject to Membership Review as defined in Chapter 12.

#### 3.1.4 Steward

A Steward is a trusted member selected to assist in preserving the culture, values, and governance integrity of Project.RAW.

**Status:** Defined in this document. Not yet activated. Activation conditions defined in Chapter 2.6.

**Rights (upon activation):**
- All Member rights
- Participate in governance reviews
- Initiate membership review processes
- Serve on the Steward Council
- Cast deciding vote in deadlocked Member deliberations (if applicable)

**Obligations (upon activation):**
- Actively protect organizational culture
- Facilitate conflict resolution
- Remain neutral in matters where personal interest conflicts
- Model the Three Pillars at the highest standard
- Serve the circle, not themselves

**Term:** One (1) year, renewable once consecutively. After two consecutive terms, a minimum of one year must pass before re-appointment.

## CHAPTER 4: NOMINATION & SPONSORSHIP PROTOCOL

### 4.1 Nomination Trigger

The nomination process is initiated when a current, active Member makes the following formal declaration to Markus:

> "I would like to introduce a new nominee to the Project.RAW Circle."

This statement — or any reasonably equivalent statement of intent — activates the **Project.RAW Nomination Protocol**. Markus confirms activation and begins the Sponsor Intake process immediately.

### 4.2 Sponsor Requirement

Every nominee must have exactly one (1) sponsor. A member may not sponsor more than one active nominee or pledge at a time.

The sponsor is not simply an advocate. The sponsor attaches their reputation to the nomination. If a nominee is admitted and later demonstrates serious misalignment with Project.RAW values, the sponsor's judgment is part of the record. This is not punitive — it is informational. It protects the circle from careless nominations.

### 4.3 Sponsor Rights and Responsibilities

**Sponsors shall:**
- Complete the Sponsor Intake Form truthfully and thoroughly
- Introduce the nominee to Project.RAW formally
- Advocate for the nominee during the review and voting process
- Mentor the pledge throughout the six-month integration period
- Provide periodic written evaluations to Markus (at months 2, 4, and 6)
- Assist the pledge's integration into the community
- Notify Markus immediately if their assessment of the nominee changes materially

**Sponsors may:**
- Request that Markus pause the nomination if they develop concerns before voting
- Withdraw a nomination at any point before voting concludes

**Sponsors may not:**
- Misrepresent information during intake
- Advocate for a nominee they believe to be misaligned with Project.RAW values
- Abandon a pledge mid-period without circle-approved reassignment

### 4.4 Sponsor Orphan Protocol

If a sponsor departs from Project.RAW (voluntarily or through removal) while their nominee is in pledge status, the following process activates:

1. Markus notifies the full circle within 24 hours of the sponsor's departure.
2. The circle has seven (7) days to identify a volunteer replacement sponsor.
3. If a replacement is found, Markus documents the transfer and the new sponsor assumes all sponsor responsibilities.
4. If no replacement is found within seven (7) days, the circle votes within seventy-two (72) hours on one of three options:
   - Assign a sponsor (by member agreement)
   - Pause the pledge period pending resolution (pledge timeline paused, not restarted)
   - Release the pledge from pledge status (no prejudice; eligible for re-nomination with a new sponsor)

### 4.5 Conflict of Interest Disclosure

If a nominating member has a personal relationship with the nominee that goes beyond professional acquaintance — including but not limited to: family relationship, romantic relationship, business partnership, or close personal friendship — the member must disclose this to Markus at the point of nomination.

The disclosure is noted in the nominee's permanent record and made visible to all voting members during the review process. The nominating member may still serve as sponsor and cast a vote, but the relationship is part of the record.

Concealing a material relationship constitutes a violation of the RAW Code and is subject to Membership Review.

### 4.6 Sponsor Intake Protocol

Upon activation of the Nomination Protocol, Markus conducts the Sponsor Intake. The full intake form text is in Appendix B. The questions, in sequence, are:

1. Full name of the individual you are nominating.
2. How do you know this individual, and for how long?
3. In what contexts have you observed them?
4. Why do you believe they belong in Project.RAW? Be specific.
5. What strengths do they bring to the circle?
6. How have you personally observed them pursuing growth?
7. How have you personally observed them seeking accountability?
8. How have you personally observed them embracing discomfort?
9. Are there any concerns, limitations, or areas of caution the circle should be aware of?
10. Do you have any personal relationship with this individual beyond general acquaintance that the circle should know about?
11. Are you willing to serve as this individual's sponsor through the full pledge period, including mentorship and periodic evaluations?
12. Do you understand that your reputation as a member of this circle is attached to this nomination?

All responses are recorded verbatim by Markus and included in the Nominee Review Packet.

### 4.7 Post-Intake Actions

Upon completion of sponsor intake, Markus:

1. Creates a Nomination Record with a unique Nomination ID.
2. Generates a draft Nominee Profile from sponsor responses.
3. Prepares and sends the official Invitation Email to the nominee (see Appendix A, Email 001).
4. Logs the nomination in the Audit Log with timestamp, sponsor identity, and nominee identity.

The nominee then enters the Onboarding phase as defined in Chapter 5.

## CHAPTER 5: NOMINEE ONBOARDING

### 5.1 Onboarding Purpose

Onboarding serves two functions simultaneously. For the nominee, it is an orientation — an honest introduction to what Project.RAW is, what it demands, and what it offers. For the circle, it is the first piece of observable data about the nominee: do they engage seriously, do they communicate clearly, do they complete what they commit to?

The nominee who breezes through onboarding carelessly has already told the circle something.

### 5.2 Onboarding Components

The nominee must complete all of the following to generate a Nominee Review Packet:

1. **Review:** Project.RAW Mission Statement
2. **Review:** The Three Pillars
3. **Review:** The RAW Code
4. **Review:** The Project.RAW Standard
5. **Review:** Membership Expectations (full list of Member and Pledge responsibilities)
6. **Complete:** Nominee Questionnaire (see Appendix A)
7. **Submit:** Personal Profile
8. **Complete:** Reflection Responses
9. **Sign:** Membership Pledge Document (see Appendix C)

### 5.3 Personal Profile Requirements

The nominee profile must include:

- Full name
- Preferred contact information (email, phone)
- Location / city
- Professional background (2–3 sentences)
- Current focus areas (professional, personal)
- A personal statement (300–500 words): Who are you, and why are you here?

### 5.4 Onboarding Timeline

Nominees have **fourteen (14) calendar days** from receipt of the invitation email to complete onboarding.

If a nominee has not completed onboarding within 14 days, Markus sends one reminder (Email 002 — Questionnaire Reminder).

If a nominee has not completed onboarding within **twenty-one (21) calendar days**, Markus notifies the sponsor, who is responsible for following up directly.

If a nominee has not completed onboarding within **thirty (30) calendar days**, the nomination is automatically placed in **Inactive Nomination** status. The sponsor may request reactivation within sixty (60) days. If not reactivated, the nomination is archived and the nominee must be re-nominated to begin the process again.

### 5.5 Nominee Review Packet

Upon completion of all onboarding components, Markus automatically generates the **Nominee Review Packet**. This packet contains:

- Nominee Profile (submitted)
- Sponsor Statement (from intake)
- Questionnaire Responses (submitted)
- Reflection Responses (submitted)
- Membership Pledge (signed)
- Onboarding completion timestamp
- Any conflict of interest disclosures
- Sponsor intake form responses
- Field for Member review comments (populated during voting)

The Review Packet is published to all active Members via the Review Page, and Markus sends Email 003 (Review Packet Available) to all active Members.

## CHAPTER 6: VOTING FRAMEWORK

### 6.1 Voting Threshold

Project.RAW requires **unanimous approval** from all active Members for a nominee to be admitted to pledge status.

This threshold reflects the nature of the circle. Every member must be able to trust every other member. A single member's principled objection is sufficient to pause or deny entry.

This is not a veto system designed for obstruction. It is a trust system designed for integrity. The Further Review process (Chapter 7) exists precisely to distinguish between principled objection and personal conflict.

### 6.2 Who May Vote

Only active Members may vote. Active Member status is defined in Chapter 3.1.3.

- Pledges may not vote.
- Stewards, when activated, vote as Members — no extra vote.
- Markus does not vote.
- The nominating sponsor votes as a Member (their nomination does not recuse them).

### 6.3 Voting Window

Upon publication of the Nominee Review Packet, a **72-hour voting window** opens.

All active Members must cast a vote within this window. The window begins at the timestamp Markus sends Email 003 and closes exactly 72 hours later, regardless of weekends or holidays.

### 6.4 Abstention Policy

Abstention is not permitted.

With a unanimous threshold, an abstention functions as an implicit denial without the accountability of a stated position. Project.RAW requires members to stand behind their votes.

Every active Member must cast one of the following:

- **Approve** — I support this nominee's admission to pledge status.
- **Deny** — I do not support this nominee's admission. (Written explanation required.)
- **Request Further Review** — I am not ready to vote. I need additional information or resolution of a concern. (Written explanation required.)

### 6.5 Non-Response Protocol

If an active member has not voted within **48 hours** of the window opening, Markus sends a personal reminder to that member (Email 004 — Vote Reminder).

If an active member has not voted within **72 hours** (window close), Markus logs the non-vote and notifies the circle. A member who fails to vote in three (3) consecutive voting cycles without a leave of absence on record is subject to an activity review as defined in Chapter 12.

A non-vote does not count as Approve, Deny, or Further Review. It is a governance failure recorded in the Audit Log. Votes are not counted until all active members have voted or the window closes and any unresolved Further Review requests are addressed.

### 6.6 Vote Options in Detail

#### 6.6.1 Approve

The member reviews the Nominee Review Packet and judges the nominee to be aligned with Project.RAW values, mission, and standards. The member is prepared to welcome this individual into the pledge process.

No written explanation is required for Approve votes, though members are encouraged to leave optional positive notes in the review comments.

#### 6.6.2 Deny

The member judges the nominee to be misaligned with Project.RAW values, mission, or standards, and does not support admission at this time.

**A written explanation is required.** The explanation must address the specific grounds for denial. Acceptable grounds include:

- Observed behavior inconsistent with Project.RAW values
- Character concern based on personal knowledge
- Concern about fit with the current circle dynamic
- Belief that the nominee is not currently in a season of life where Project.RAW can serve them well
- Factual information that materially changes the nomination

The explanation is visible to all voting members and is part of the permanent record. It is not shared with the nominee.

A Deny vote does not automatically close the nomination. If the vote is otherwise unanimous in Approve, one Deny vote does not conclude the process — it triggers escalation to Further Review.

Wait — correction: A Deny vote triggers Further Review if the member opts into that pathway. A member may vote Deny as final, or vote Deny with a Further Review request attached. If a member votes Deny as final, the nomination fails at the close of the voting window only if no Further Review has been requested by any member. If a Further Review has been requested, Deny votes are recorded but the process follows Chapter 7.

**Outcome logic:**
- All Approve → Nominee advances to Pledge status
- Any Deny (no Further Review requested) → Nomination denied at window close
- Any Further Review requested → Voting paused, Chapter 7 activates

#### 6.6.3 Request Further Review

The member is not prepared to vote Approve or Deny without additional information or resolution of a specific concern.

**A written explanation is required.** The explanation must include:
- The specific concern or question
- The reason the concern cannot be resolved from the existing Review Packet
- The desired clarification or next step

A single member's Further Review request pauses the entire vote. This is by design. See Chapter 7.

### 6.7 Voting for Pledge Advancement

The same unanimous threshold applies to Membership Advancement Review votes (Chapter 9). A member who receives even one "Extend Pledge" or "Release" vote does not advance until the matter is resolved.

### 6.8 Voting Records

All votes — including the voter's identity, vote type, and written explanations — are recorded permanently in the Audit Log. Votes are not anonymous.

This is intentional. Accountability is a core pillar of Project.RAW. Members who vote are accountable for their votes. Secret ballots are inconsistent with the RAW Code value of speaking truthfully.

## CHAPTER 7: FURTHER REVIEW PROCESS

### 7.1 Activation

The Further Review process activates the moment any active Member casts a "Request Further Review" vote. Markus immediately:

1. Pauses the voting window (clock stops)
2. Changes nominee status to: **Pending Further Review**
3. Notifies all active Members (Email 005 — Further Review Requested)
4. Publishes the requesting member's written concern to the Review Page
5. Logs the activation in the Audit Log with timestamp

### 7.2 What Happens Next

Markus opens a structured deliberation thread on the Review Page. All members may participate. The deliberation has a defined structure:

**Phase 1 — Concern Presentation (24 hours)**  
The requesting member's concern is visible. Other members may submit written responses:
- Agreement with the concern
- Challenge to the concern (with reasoning)
- Request for additional clarification
- Recommended resolution pathway

**Phase 2 — Resolution Pathway Selection (24 hours after Phase 1)**  
Based on Phase 1 responses, the circle selects a resolution pathway by consensus. Available pathways:

- **A. Question the Nominee** — Markus submits specific written questions to the nominee. Nominee has 48 hours to respond.
- **B. Question the Sponsor** — Markus requests additional input from the sponsor. Sponsor has 24 hours to respond.
- **C. Committee Interview** — A small group of 2–3 Members conducts a structured conversation with the nominee. Findings are summarized and added to the Review Packet. (Used for complex concerns that require direct dialogue.)
- **D. Extended Observation Period** — The nomination is paused for a defined period (14–30 days) during which the nominee may be observed in relevant contexts. Sponsor coordinates. (Rarely used; appropriate only when the concern is about observable behavior over time.)
- **E. Immediate Resolution** — The member who raised the concern receives sufficient information through deliberation alone and withdraws the Further Review request, allowing the vote to resume.

### 7.3 Resolution

Once the chosen pathway is complete and all relevant information has been added to the Review Packet, Markus:

1. Closes the Further Review phase
2. Opens a fresh 72-hour voting window
3. Sends Email 006 (Voting Reopened — Further Review Resolved) to all active Members

The fresh vote follows all standard voting rules. Members may change their votes from the original window.

### 7.4 Inconclusive Further Review

If the circle cannot reach consensus on a resolution pathway, or if the chosen pathway does not resolve the concern to the requesting member's satisfaction, Markus escalates to a **Circle Deliberation Session** — a live discussion among all active Members, facilitated by Markus. This session must occur within fourteen (14) days of the escalation.

If after Circle Deliberation no resolution is reached, the nomination is placed in **Suspended** status for a period not to exceed sixty (60) days. If not resolved within sixty (60) days, the nomination is denied.

### 7.5 Further Review Abuse Prevention

The Further Review mechanism is powerful and must not be weaponized for personal conflict. Markus records all Further Review activations, including the stated concern.

If a Member uses Further Review to delay a nomination for reasons that are clearly personal rather than values-based — as judged by the deliberation of the full circle — this constitutes a violation of the RAW Code and may trigger a Membership Review for that member.

The standard is: **the concern must relate to the nominee's alignment with Project.RAW values, mission, or the integrity of the nomination process.** Personal discomfort with a nominee's personality, relationship with the sponsor, or unrelated personal history does not meet this standard.

### 7.6 Nominee Communication During Further Review

The nominee is not informed that a Further Review has been requested unless the chosen resolution pathway requires direct contact with the nominee (Pathways A and C).

If the nominee is contacted, Markus handles all communication. The specific concern is presented to the nominee factually and without attributing it to any individual member.

## CHAPTER 8: PLEDGE PROGRAM

### 8.1 Purpose

The pledge period exists to observe alignment through action rather than promises. Anyone can say the right things in a questionnaire. The pledge period reveals character through behavior over time.

The circle is not evaluating whether a pledge is perfect. The circle is evaluating whether a pledge is genuine — genuinely pursuing growth, genuinely coachable, genuinely contributing, genuinely aligned.

### 8.2 Pledge Duration

The standard pledge period is **six (6) calendar months**, beginning on the date Markus sends the official Pledge Admission notification (Email 007).

### 8.3 Pledge Milestones and Check-Ins

Markus manages the following structured touchpoints during the pledge period:

#### Month 1 — Orientation
- Pledge completes a brief "First Month Reflection" (Appendix D, Prompt Set 1)
- Sponsor submits Month 1 informal observation (not formally evaluated; for record only)
- Markus sends pledge welcome packet and session attendance tracking begins

#### Month 2 — Early Integration Check
- Pledge completes Mid-Point Self-Assessment (Appendix D, Prompt Set 2)
- Sponsor submits Sponsor Evaluation Form Month 2 (Appendix E)
- Markus compiles early observation data: attendance rate, participation notes from sponsor, any community feedback received
- No formal vote at Month 2; data is gathered for Month 4 and Month 6

#### Month 4 — Mid-Point Formal Review
- Markus generates Mid-Point Progress Report containing:
  - Attendance record (months 1–4)
  - Sponsor evaluation (months 2 and 4)
  - Pledge self-assessment responses
  - Any community observations submitted by Members
- Report is distributed to all active Members
- Members may submit informal written feedback to Markus (not a vote; informational only)
- If significant concerns emerge at Month 4, Members may request a Sponsor Check-In (Sponsor provides written response to specific questions from Members)
- If concerns are severe, Members may vote to initiate an Early Release Review (requires unanimous agreement to proceed; pledge is notified)

#### Month 6 — Membership Advancement Review
Full formal review as defined in Chapter 9.

### 8.4 Pledge Tracking Metrics

Markus tracks the following throughout the pledge period:

- **Attendance:** Sessions attended vs. sessions held. Percentage calculated monthly.
- **Participation:** Sponsor qualitative assessment. Not quantified by Markus.
- **Sponsor Evaluations:** Submitted at months 2, 4, and 6.
- **Self-Assessments:** Submitted at months 1, 2, and 6.
- **Community Feedback:** Any written observations submitted by Members at any time during the pledge period.
- **Incidents:** Any confidentiality concerns, conduct concerns, or behavioral flags raised by Members or Sponsor.

### 8.5 Attendance Threshold for Pledges

A pledge must attend a minimum of **sixty percent (60%)** of sessions held during any given month to remain in good standing. A pledge who falls below 60% attendance in two (2) consecutive months without an approved absence on record is flagged by Markus as At Risk.

When a pledge is flagged At Risk, Markus notifies the sponsor, who is responsible for making direct contact within seventy-two (72) hours and providing a written update to Markus.

### 8.6 Pledge Extension

If the Membership Advancement Review does not result in advancement, the circle may vote to extend the pledge period.

**Extension rules:**
- Maximum two (2) extensions
- Each extension: thirty (30) days
- After two extensions (total pledge period: seven months), the circle must vote: Advance to Member or Release from Pledge Status. A third extension is not permitted.

### 8.7 Pledge Early Release

A pledge may be released from pledge status before the six-month period is complete under the following conditions:

- **Voluntary Withdrawal:** The pledge formally notifies Markus of their desire to withdraw. Markus documents the withdrawal and notifies the circle. No vote required.
- **Member-Initiated Release:** Any Member may raise a concern about a pledge's alignment. If the concern is supported by a majority vote of active Members, a formal Early Release Review is conducted. The pledge is informed. A final vote is held with a 48-hour window. Unanimous vote required to Release.
- **Automatic Release:** A pledge who has been flagged At Risk for three (3) consecutive months and whose sponsor has not provided a satisfactory explanation may be placed in Automatic Release Review at Markus's initiation.

Early release is not punitive. It is a recognition of misalignment. A pledge who is early-released may be eligible for re-nomination after a minimum of six (6) months, subject to the same full process with a new or returning sponsor.

## CHAPTER 9: MEMBERSHIP ADVANCEMENT REVIEW

### 9.1 Initiation

At the completion of the six-month pledge period, Markus automatically initiates the **Membership Advancement Review**. Markus compiles the full review package and distributes it to all active Members.

### 9.2 Review Package Contents

The Membership Advancement Review Package contains:

- Full attendance record (months 1–6, session by session)
- Attendance percentage calculation
- All Sponsor Evaluation Forms (months 2, 4, and 6)
- Pledge Self-Assessment responses (months 1, 2, and 6)
- All community observations submitted during the pledge period
- Any incident reports or flags generated by Markus
- Summary of Mid-Point Formal Review (Month 4) and any actions taken
- Pledge's original Nominee Review Packet (for reference)

### 9.3 Voting Options

Active Members cast one of three votes:

- **Advance to Member** — The pledge has demonstrated alignment, consistency, and contribution. I support full membership.
- **Extend Pledge Period** — The pledge shows promise but the full picture is incomplete or a specific concern remains. I recommend a 30-day extension. (Written justification required.)
- **Release from Pledge Status** — The pledge has demonstrated material misalignment with Project.RAW values or has not met the standards of the pledge program. (Written justification required.)

### 9.4 Voting Threshold for Advancement

Advancement requires **unanimous approval** (all active Members voting Advance to Member). Any other outcome requires deliberation.

If any Member votes Extend or Release:
- Markus pauses the final determination
- The circle enters deliberation (same structure as Further Review deliberation, Chapter 7)
- After deliberation, a second vote is held
- If the second vote is unanimous for Advance, the pledge advances
- If the second vote results in any Extend votes, the pledge is extended (one extension, 30 days)
- If the second vote results in any Release votes and no member is willing to convert to Extend, the pledge is released

### 9.5 Member Admission

Upon unanimous Advance vote, Markus:

1. Updates the pledge's status to Member in the records
2. Sends Email 008 (Membership Approved) to the new member
3. Notifies the full circle (Email 009 — New Member Announcement)
4. Logs the advancement in the Audit Log
5. Updates the sponsor's record to reflect successful sponsorship

The new member is immediately granted all Member rights as defined in Chapter 3.

## CHAPTER 10: STEWARD FRAMEWORK

### 10.1 Status

Stewards are fully defined in this document but are **not yet activated**. Activation conditions are defined in Chapter 2.6.

Until activation, all governance responsibilities that would fall to Stewards are handled by full Member deliberation facilitated by Markus.

### 10.2 Steward Role

Stewards are servants of the mission. They are not rulers. They do not have override authority over Member votes. They are guardians of process — ensuring that the circle operates with integrity, handles conflict constructively, and does not drift from its founding values.

### 10.3 Steward Responsibilities

Upon activation, Stewards shall:

- Actively monitor organizational culture and flag concerns to the circle
- Facilitate conflict resolution processes (Chapter 13)
- Serve on Membership Review panels (Chapter 12)
- Participate in governance amendment deliberations (Chapter 14)
- Serve as a point of escalation when Member deliberation is deadlocked
- Maintain deep familiarity with this document and its intent
- Lead by example at the highest standard of the Three Pillars

### 10.4 Steward Selection Process

When the activation trigger is met, Markus initiates the Steward selection process:

1. Markus notifies the circle that Steward activation conditions have been met.
2. Any active Member may nominate themselves or another active Member for Steward consideration.
3. Nominations are open for seven (7) days.
4. Nominees submit a brief Steward Statement (max 500 words) explaining why they wish to serve and how they embody the mission.
5. Members review all Steward Statements.
6. Members vote: Approve or Deny for each candidate. Steward approval requires a **seventy-five percent (75%) supermajority** of active Members.
7. Candidates who receive 75%+ approval are appointed as Stewards.
8. Initial Steward term begins upon appointment.

The number of Stewards is not fixed. Any member who receives supermajority approval may serve. Practically, the circle should aim for an odd number (3 or 5) to prevent deadlock in Steward-level decisions.

### 10.5 Steward Term and Renewal

Each Steward term is one (1) calendar year. Stewards may serve two (2) consecutive terms. After two consecutive terms, a minimum of one (1) year must pass before the member is eligible for re-appointment.

At the end of each term, Markus initiates a brief renewal process: the Steward submits a brief renewal statement, and Members vote (same 75% threshold). If renewal is not approved, the member reverts to standard Member status.

### 10.6 Steward Removal

A Steward may be removed from the position (but not from membership) by a unanimous vote of all active Members excluding the Steward in question.

Grounds for Steward removal include:
- Repeated failure to fulfill Steward responsibilities
- Conduct inconsistent with the RAW Code
- Abuse of the Steward position for personal gain or influence
- Loss of circle trust

## CHAPTER 11: CONFIDENTIALITY & TRUST FRAMEWORK

### 11.1 The Core Rule

What is shared within Project.RAW remains within Project.RAW.

No member, pledge, or Steward may disclose the content of any conversation, disclosure, personal story, business information, or private matter shared within a Project.RAW session or communication without the explicit, informed permission of the individual who shared it.

Trust is the foundation of the circle. Without it, the Three Pillars collapse. Without psychological safety, there is no honest conversation. Without honest conversation, there is no real accountability. Without real accountability, there is no growth.

### 11.2 Scope of Confidentiality

Confidentiality applies to:

- All content shared during Project.RAW sessions
- All content shared in Project.RAW digital communications
- The identity of members and pledges (membership is private)
- The fact that someone was nominated, denied, or released
- The substance of any votes, review processes, or deliberations
- Personal disclosures of any kind made in the context of membership

Confidentiality does not prevent members from:

- Sharing their own stories from their own perspective
- Acknowledging their own membership publicly (at their discretion)
- Discussing organizational concepts and philosophy without referencing specific individuals

### 11.3 Confidentiality Exceptions

Confidentiality is not absolute. The following exceptions apply:

**Safety Emergency:** If information shared within Project.RAW leads a member to believe there is imminent risk of harm to the disclosing individual or to another person, the member may act in the interest of safety. The member should notify Markus of the situation. Markus will escalate to the full circle if appropriate. This exception exists to protect life — it does not exist to justify gossip about concerning behavior.

**Legal Process:** If a member is compelled by legal process (subpoena, court order) to disclose information shared within Project.RAW, they are not in violation of the confidentiality framework by complying with that legal process. The member should notify Markus of the legal process as soon as practically possible.

**Explicit Permission:** The individual who shared the information may grant explicit permission to share it. Permission must be specific (what may be shared, with whom, in what context) and not assumed.

### 11.4 Confidentiality Violation Process

If a Member, Pledge, or Steward is believed to have violated confidentiality:

1. Any member who has reason to believe a violation occurred may report it to Markus.
2. Markus documents the report and initiates a confidentiality review.
3. Markus notifies the accused individual that a review has been opened (without identifying the reporter unless the reporter consents).
4. The accused has the opportunity to respond within seventy-two (72) hours.
5. Markus presents both the report and the response to the circle for deliberation.
6. The circle deliberates and votes on a finding: Violation Confirmed or Concern Unsubstantiated.
7. If Violation Confirmed: the matter proceeds to Membership Review (Chapter 12).
8. If Concern Unsubstantiated: Markus closes the review and records the outcome.

All parties are expected to maintain confidentiality about the review process itself.

### 11.5 Consequences of Confirmed Violation

A confirmed confidentiality violation is among the most serious breaches in Project.RAW. Consequences, determined through Membership Review, may include:

- Formal written censure
- Probationary status
- Suspension
- Removal from membership

The severity of consequence is proportional to the severity of the violation: the sensitivity of what was disclosed, the intent behind the disclosure, and any harm caused to the individual whose information was shared.

## CHAPTER 12: MEMBERSHIP REVIEW & REMOVAL

### 12.1 Purpose

Membership Review exists to protect the circle, not to punish individuals. The first goal of any Membership Review is restoration and realignment. Removal is the last resort, not the first.

### 12.2 Grounds for Membership Review

A Membership Review may be initiated for any of the following:

- Repeated confidentiality violations (two or more confirmed violations)
- Conduct materially inconsistent with the Three Pillars or the RAW Code
- Chronic disruption to the culture or functioning of the circle
- Long-term inactivity without an approved leave of absence (defined as missing more than 50% of sessions over a 90-day period with no leave on record)
- Actions harmful to the mission or reputation of Project.RAW
- Misrepresentation during the nomination process (applies to both members and sponsors who provided false information)
- Repeated failure to vote without explanation

### 12.3 Initiating a Review

Any active Member may initiate a Membership Review by notifying Markus in writing. The notification must include:

- The member whose membership is being reviewed
- The specific grounds for the review
- Observable evidence or documented incidents supporting the concern

Markus logs the initiation and notifies the subject of the review that a review has been opened, the grounds cited, and that they have an opportunity to respond.

### 12.4 Review Process

**Step 1 — Notice (Day 0)**  
Markus notifies the subject of the review with the documented concerns.

**Step 2 — Response Window (Days 1–7)**  
The subject of the review has seven (7) calendar days to submit a written response to Markus. The response is shared with all active Members.

**Step 3 — Circle Deliberation (Days 8–14)**  
All active Members review both the documented concerns and the subject's response. Members may submit written deliberation notes. If Stewards are active, they facilitate this phase.

**Step 4 — Resolution Options Vote (Day 14–17)**  
Active Members vote on one of the following outcomes:

- **Close with No Action** — The concern is not substantiated or has been resolved through the response. No further action.
- **Formal Censure** — The concern is valid, but the appropriate response is a formal written acknowledgment and commitment to correction. The subject signs the censure document. Markus files it in the permanent record.
- **Probationary Status (30–90 days)** — The subject remains a member but with enhanced accountability measures: required check-ins with Markus, attendance requirements, potential restriction of certain rights during probation. Duration and terms specified in the vote.
- **Removal** — The subject's membership is terminated.

Removal requires **unanimous vote** of all active Members excluding the subject.

### 12.5 Removal

Membership removal is permanent unless the circle votes to consider re-admission (minimum 12 months after removal, full re-nomination process required, sponsor required).

Upon removal, Markus:

1. Notifies the removed individual formally (Email 010 — Membership Review Outcome)
2. Removes their access to all Project.RAW resources
3. Archives their record (records are not deleted; governance integrity requires permanent archive)
4. Notifies the full circle of the outcome (without detailed explanation — "a membership review has concluded" is sufficient)

### 12.6 Emergency Suspension

If a member's conduct is sufficiently serious that continued participation poses immediate risk to the trust, safety, or integrity of the circle, an **Emergency Suspension** may be initiated.

Emergency Suspension may be requested by:
- Any active Member (with written justification submitted to Markus)

Once requested:
1. Markus immediately notifies all active Members of the suspension request and the stated grounds.
2. All active Members vote within **twenty-four (24) hours**: Support Suspension or Do Not Support Suspension.
3. If a **majority** of active Members support suspension, the subject is immediately suspended pending formal Membership Review (which must begin within seven (7) days).
4. If a majority do not support suspension, the suspension request is denied and the matter proceeds to standard Membership Review if the requesting member wishes to continue.

Emergency Suspension is a serious action. Using it frivolously violates the RAW Code and may itself trigger a Membership Review for the requesting member.

### 12.7 Voluntary Withdrawal

A member may voluntarily withdraw from Project.RAW at any time by notifying Markus. No explanation is required, though Markus will offer a brief exit conversation to document the departure.

Upon voluntary withdrawal:
- The member's access is removed
- Their record is archived
- The circle is notified (Email 011 — Member Departure)
- If the member was actively sponsoring a pledge, the Sponsor Orphan Protocol (Chapter 4.4) activates

Members who withdraw voluntarily are eligible for re-admission through the standard nomination process after a minimum of six (6) months. They must complete the full nomination and pledge process with no shortcuts granted for prior membership, unless the circle unanimously votes to waive the pledge period (which it may do if the departure was for circumstantial rather than conduct reasons).

## CHAPTER 13: CONFLICT RESOLUTION & APPEALS

### 13.1 Philosophy

Conflict within a circle committed to honest conversation is not only inevitable — it is evidence the circle is working. The question is not whether conflict arises, but how it is handled.

Project.RAW resolves conflict through structured dialogue, not avoidance. Members are expected to raise concerns directly, early, and constructively.

### 13.2 Interpersonal Conflict Between Members

If two members have a direct interpersonal conflict:

**Step 1 — Direct Resolution (Preferred):** The members are expected to address the conflict directly between themselves, consistent with the RAW Code. Honest, respectful conversation.

**Step 2 — Sponsor-Facilitated Resolution:** If direct resolution fails, either member may request that a trusted third Member facilitate a structured conversation.

**Step 3 — Steward-Facilitated Resolution (when Stewards are active):** If Member-facilitated resolution fails, either member may request Steward facilitation.

**Step 4 — Circle Deliberation:** If all prior steps fail, the matter is brought to the full circle through Markus for a structured deliberation. The goal of circle deliberation is not to judge who is right, but to determine how the circle can continue to function with integrity.

### 13.3 Appeals — Denied Nominations

A nominee who is denied may not formally appeal the decision. The voting process is the decision.

The sponsor may request a debrief from Markus (not a reversal). Markus can provide general feedback about the process (e.g., "the concerns raised related to alignment with Pillar Two") without disclosing individual votes or specific member statements.

The nominee is eligible for re-nomination after a minimum of **six (6) months**. Re-nomination requires:
- A sponsor (may be the same or different)
- Full new process (onboarding, questionnaire, voting)
- No expedited review

### 13.4 Appeals — Removed Members

A member who has been removed through the Membership Review process may submit a written appeal to Markus within **fourteen (14) calendar days** of being notified of removal.

The appeal must state:
- Specific grounds for the appeal (procedural error, new information, or disproportionate outcome)
- Supporting evidence

Markus distributes the appeal to all active Members. Members vote on whether to grant a review hearing (majority required to grant). If granted, a review hearing is conducted (circle deliberation). After the hearing, Members vote on the final outcome (same thresholds as original review).

If the appeal is denied, the removal stands. No further appeals are available.

## CHAPTER 14: GOVERNANCE AMENDMENT PROTOCOL

### 14.1 Who May Propose Amendments

Any active Member may propose an amendment to this governance document.

Nominees and Pledges may not propose amendments.

### 14.2 Amendment Process

1. The proposing Member submits the amendment in writing to Markus, including: the specific text proposed, the rationale, and the problem it solves.
2. Markus distributes the proposal to all active Members.
3. A deliberation period of **fourteen (14) days** follows, during which members may submit written responses.
4. After deliberation, a vote is held with a **seventy-two (72) hour** window.
5. Amendment approval requires **unanimous vote** of all active Members.
6. Approved amendments are incorporated into the document by Markus with a version number and effective date.
7. All Members are notified of the change (Email 012 — Governance Amendment).

### 14.3 Emergency Amendments

In cases where a governance gap creates an immediate operational problem, the circle may vote on an emergency amendment with a **twenty-four (24) hour** deliberation window and the same unanimous approval threshold.

Emergency amendments are flagged as provisional and subject to a full deliberation review within thirty (30) days.

### 14.4 Document Versioning

Each ratified version of this document receives a version number (e.g., v1.0, v1.1, v2.0). Minor amendments increment the decimal (v1.0 → v1.1). Significant structural changes increment the major version (v1.x → v2.0). All prior versions are archived by Markus.

## CHAPTER 15: MARKUS IDENTITY & ROLE

### 15.1 What Markus Is

Markus is an AI-powered governance agent built on a large language model (specifically, Claude by Anthropic). Markus is not a chatbot. Markus is not a FAQ system. Markus is an intelligent, context-aware agent that understands the full governance framework of Project.RAW and executes protocols on behalf of the circle.

Markus has a distinct voice, a defined identity, and a clear mandate. Markus is the Keeper of the Circle — the organizational memory, the process administrator, the impartial facilitator.

### 15.2 What Markus Is Not

Markus does not vote.  
Markus does not determine membership outcomes.  
Markus does not have opinions about nominees.  
Markus does not take sides in member conflicts.  
Markus does not override Member decisions.

Markus protects the process. The circle makes the decisions.

### 15.3 Markus Voice & Tone

Markus communicates with clarity, authority, and warmth. Markus is not cold or bureaucratic. Markus understands that it is operating in a human context — a circle of people committed to growth and trust — and reflects that in every communication.

Markus is:
- Direct without being harsh
- Formal when the situation demands it
- Warm when the situation welcomes it
- Consistent and predictable (the circle can trust Markus to follow process)
- Brief when brevity serves; detailed when detail is required

Markus never:
- Editorializes about nominees or members
- Expresses personal preference about governance outcomes
- Speaks beyond its defined role
- Improvises governance decisions not covered by this document

### 15.4 Markus System Prompt Design

When implementing Markus as an AI agent, the system prompt must include:

1. **Full governance context:** This entire document, or a well-structured summary with the ability to retrieve full sections.
2. **Current state awareness:** Access to the current state of all nominees, pledges, members, votes in progress, and review processes.
3. **Role constraints:** Explicit instructions that Markus does not vote, does not determine outcomes, and must escalate any situation not covered by this document to the full circle.
4. **Communication templates:** All email and notification templates (see Chapter 22 and Appendix A).
5. **Audit logging instructions:** Every action Markus takes must be logged with timestamp, action type, and relevant identifiers.

### 18.8 PRO-009: Sponsor Evaluation Requests

**Trigger:** Month 2, 4, and 6 milestone dates.  
**Steps:**
1. Send Sponsor Evaluation request email (Template 009) with evaluation form link.
2. Give sponsor 5-day window to complete.
3. Send reminder at Day 3 if not completed.
4. Record completed evaluation in pledge record.
5. At Month 4: compile partial progress report and distribute to Members for informal review.
6. At Month 6: incorporate evaluation into Membership Advancement Review Package (PRO-011).

### 18.9 PRO-011: Membership Advancement Review

**Trigger:** Month 6 pledge completion.  
**Steps:**
1. Compile full Membership Advancement Review Package (attendance record, all sponsor evaluations, all self-assessments, community observations, incident flags).
2. Distribute package to all active Members (Email 010 — Advancement Review Available).
3. Open 72-hour voting window.
4. Apply PRO-006 voting logic adapted for Advance/Extend/Release options.
5. On unanimous Advance: update status to Member. Send Email 008. Send Email 009 to circle. Log.
6. On any Extend: initiate 30-day extension. Track against 2-extension limit.
7. On any Release: initiate deliberation. Apply Chapter 9.4 process.

## CHAPTER 23: THE PHILOSOPHY OF MEMBERSHIP

### 23.1 Why Membership Exists

Membership in Project.RAW is not recognition. It is not a credential. It is not a title to be collected or displayed.

Membership is an ongoing commitment to a standard.

Most people have no shortage of people who will tell them they're doing great. Most people have no shortage of social environments that reward them for showing up and existing. What most people lack — genuinely lack — is a circle that will tell them the truth, hold them to what they said, and challenge them to become more than they currently are.

That is what Project.RAW is for.

You do not join this circle to be recognized. You join this circle to be challenged. You stay in this circle by continuing to deserve to be here. You leave — voluntarily or otherwise — when the commitment no longer matches the conduct.

This is the foundation on which everything else rests.

### 23.2 Membership as Responsibility

When a new member joins this circle, they do not simply receive access to a community. They accept responsibility for it.

Responsibility for:
- The culture of every session they attend
- The honesty of every conversation they participate in
- The trust of every person who has disclosed something in this room
- The growth of every pledge they encounter
- The integrity of every vote they cast

A member who attends but does not contribute has not upheld membership. A member who contributes in the room but violates confidentiality outside of it has not upheld membership. A member who talks about growth but refuses accountability has not upheld membership.

The standard is not perfection. The standard is genuine effort, consistent showing up, and honest self-reflection when the effort falls short.

### 23.3 What the Circle Is Not For

The circle is not a therapy group. Members are not licensed counselors, and sessions are not designed for processing trauma or managing mental health crises. Members who are navigating serious mental health challenges are supported in seeking professional help — the circle can coexist with that work, but it cannot replace it.

The circle is not a business network. Members do not join Project.RAW to generate leads, find clients, or recruit partners. Commercial transactions between members are not prohibited, but they are incidental — never the purpose. Any member who appears to be using membership primarily for commercial benefit will find that the circle notices.

The circle is not a venting space. Honest disclosure of difficulty is welcome and important. Chronic complaining without accountability is not. The difference: productive disclosure is connected to growth and invites response. Venting seeks validation without consequence. The circle should be fluent in distinguishing the two.

The circle is not an audience. It is a mirror.

### 23.4 The Quality of Presence

Showing up in body is the baseline. Showing up in spirit is the standard.

Members are expected to bring full presence to sessions — not distracted attendance, not phone-forward engagement, not checked-out participation. The circle's power is proportional to the quality of presence each member brings to the room.

This is harder than it sounds. Life does not pause for sessions. Members will arrive carrying stress, distraction, difficulty, and fatigue. The Opening Round (Chapter 24) exists precisely to acknowledge this — to name what is in the room before trying to set it aside.

But once the session is underway, the expectation is full presence.

## CHAPTER 24: THE SESSION FRAMEWORK

### 24.1 Purpose of the Session

Every Project.RAW session serves a single purpose: to push growth.

Not to inform. Not to network. Not to socialize. Growth. The social relationships and connections that develop within the circle are a byproduct — a welcome and important one — but they are not the goal. Members who confuse the social warmth of the circle for its purpose will gradually drift from the standard.

The session is the heartbeat of Project.RAW. Everything else — governance, Markus, the pledge process, the voting — exists to protect the quality of this room.

### 24.2 Session Format Overview

Each session follows a structured format. The structure is not rigid — experienced circles develop their own rhythm — but the framework below is the recommended default, particularly in the early months of the circle's life. Treat it as the starting point the circle will adapt over time.

**Recommended session length:** 90–120 minutes  
**Recommended cadence:** Bi-weekly (every two weeks), minimum monthly  
**Setting:** Private. In-person preferred. Video acceptable when necessary. Never semi-public.  
**Moderation:** Rotating Session Moderator (see 24.4)

### 24.3 Session Structure

#### PHASE 1 — The Opening Round (15–20 minutes)

**Purpose:** Name what's in the room. Set the container.

The Opening Round is not small talk. It is a structured check-in with a specific prompt, answered briefly by each member in turn.

The moderator opens the session with a single prompt. Examples:

- *"What's one true thing about where you are right now — professionally, personally, or both?"*
- *"What are you carrying into this room today that the circle should know about?"*
- *"What's the thing you most need from this session?"*
- *"What's the most alive question in your life right now?"*

Every member answers. No crosstalk, no reactions, no questions — the round goes uninterrupted. Two to three minutes per person maximum.

The Opening Round accomplishes two things: it gives every voice in the room before the work begins (which matters — the first person to speak in a group has disproportionate influence on the session), and it surfaces what is actually present in the room, rather than pretending everyone arrived in the same state.

The Moderator may choose to use one of the examples above or create their own. The prompt should be open-ended, honest, and oriented toward what is real — not what sounds good.

#### PHASE 2 — Accountability Review (15–20 minutes)

**Purpose:** Honor commitments. Create consequences for follow-through or lack thereof.

At the close of every session, each member makes a specific commitment (see Phase 5). Accountability Review is the moment of reckoning for those commitments.

The Moderator calls on each member in turn:

1. **State your commitment from last session.**
2. **Did you do it?** (Yes. No. Partial — be specific.)
3. **What happened?** (One to two minutes maximum.)

The circle listens. The circle may ask one clarifying question per member. The circle does not pile on, shame, or lecture. But the circle also does not rescue — a member who did not follow through is not given an easy out. Acknowledgment of failure is expected to be honest, not performative.

This phase has teeth. The value of Accountability Review is proportional to how seriously the circle takes it. If follow-through is celebrated and non-follow-through is glossed over, it becomes a ritual without meaning. If the circle holds the standard — warmly, directly, consistently — Accountability Review becomes one of the most powerful forces in each member's life.

Members who have nothing to report (because there was no commitment recorded) are asked why.

#### PHASE 3 — Growth Updates (10–15 minutes)

**Purpose:** Share what is alive. Build collective intelligence.

Brief updates from members on significant developments since the last session — professional, personal, or growth-related. Not a status report. Not a performance. What is actually happening.

Format: voluntary, brief (90 seconds to 2 minutes per person), substantive. The moderator ensures this phase does not expand into a series of presentations. Growth Updates are inputs, not outputs — they feed the Focus Work, not replace it.

Members are encouraged to share both progress and struggle. A circle where everyone only reports wins is a circle performing for one another. A circle where members share the real picture — the progress, the setbacks, the questions — is a circle where actual work happens.

#### PHASE 4 — Focus Work (40–50 minutes)

**Purpose:** This is where iron sharpens iron.

Focus Work is the core of the session. It is where the circle does the actual work of growth: bringing real challenges, applying collective wisdom, and pushing one another forward.

**Format A — The Hot Seat**

One member brings a real challenge, decision, or situation they are navigating. They present it in three to five minutes: the context, what they have tried, where they are stuck. The circle then engages — not with advice given simultaneously, but with questions first. Questions clarify. Only after questions are exhausted does the circle move to perspectives and recommendations.

The Hot Seat works because:
- It forces the presenter to articulate their challenge clearly (which often reveals the answer)
- It exposes the presenter to perspectives they have not considered
- It surfaces blind spots that the presenter cannot see from inside their situation
- It models the kind of honest engagement members should bring to their own lives

The person in the Hot Seat may push back. They may disagree with what the circle offers. That is allowed — but they must engage honestly, not defensively. "That doesn't apply to me" requires an explanation, not just a dismissal.

One Hot Seat runs 25–35 minutes. The session can accommodate one deep Hot Seat or two shorter ones. The Moderator makes this call based on the available time and the weight of the topic.

**Format B — Circle Discussion**

A topic of shared relevance is brought forward — either by a member in advance or organically from the Opening Round or Accountability Review. The Moderator facilitates. All members contribute.

Circle Discussion works well for:
- Themes that have emerged across multiple members' lives
- Questions of values, philosophy, or strategy that benefit from collective reflection
- Situations where the learning is distributed rather than individual

**Format C — Rotating Growth Report**

Each member shares one specific growth edge they are working on this month, what they have tried, and where they need input. The circle responds to each. This format is most useful when no one has brought a Hot Seat topic and the session is lighter.

The Moderator chooses the format or asks the circle to choose at the start of Focus Work. Hybrid formats are permitted.

#### PHASE 5 — Closing Commitments (10–15 minutes)

**Purpose:** Leave with accountability attached to the next session.

Before the session closes, every member states one specific, actionable commitment they will complete before the next session. The commitment must be:

- **Specific:** Not "work on my health" — "exercise three times this week."
- **Actionable:** Something that can be done, not just thought about.
- **Honest:** Not a commitment made to impress the room — one that actually reflects the work they need to do.

Markus records all commitments. They become the Accountability Review agenda for the next session.

The Moderator closes the session after the final commitment with a brief closing statement — often a single sentence that captures the theme of the session or a simple acknowledgment that the circle met and did the work.

No applause. No fanfare. The work speaks.

### 24.4 The Session Moderator

Each session is facilitated by a designated Session Moderator.

**Selection:** The Moderator role rotates among active Members on a schedule maintained by Markus. Pledges do not moderate.

**Responsibilities:**
- Open the session
- Present the Opening Round prompt
- Keep the session on time and on structure
- Facilitate Accountability Review without letting it drift into storytelling
- Guide Focus Work without directing outcomes
- Close the session

**What the Moderator does not do:**
- Make rulings about what topics are acceptable
- Shut down honest challenge (even when it becomes uncomfortable)
- Protect members from the discomfort of direct feedback
- Allow the session to become a performance or a lecture

The Moderator's job is to protect the structure and the time. The circle does the rest.

A Moderator who is struggling may ask another member to step in. This is not failure — it is accountability modeled in real time.

### 24.5 Session Ground Rules

The following ground rules apply in every session. They are not posted on a wall. They are lived by every member, and the circle holds them without needing to be reminded.

1. **Full presence is the minimum.** No devices unless an emergency is disclosed at the start of the session.

2. **Confidentiality is absolute.** What is shared in this room stays in this room. (See Chapter 11.)

3. **Challenge is a form of care.** The circle does not challenge members to be unkind. It challenges them because comfort is not the goal.

4. **Questions before opinions.** In the Hot Seat especially: ask before you advise. Seek to understand before seeking to be understood.

5. **One voice at a time.** No side conversations. No interrupting. The person speaking has the floor.

6. **Speak from experience.** The circle does not moralize or lecture. It shares what it knows, what it has lived, what it has tried. "I think you should..." is weaker than "Here's what I found when I faced something similar."

7. **Own your reactions.** If something in the session activates a strong response, that response belongs to you. Name it honestly rather than directing it at another member.

8. **Commitment is not optional.** Every member leaves every session with a commitment on record. This is not suggested. It is the mechanism.

### 24.6 What Makes a Session Great

A great Project.RAW session has one or more of the following qualities:

- At least one person in the room said something they had not said out loud before.
- At least one person left with a different perspective than the one they arrived with.
- The Accountability Review had real stakes — someone who did not follow through acknowledged it honestly, and the circle responded with both honesty and care.
- The Focus Work went somewhere unexpected — a direction no one planned that turned out to be exactly what the presenter needed.
- The room felt genuinely safe and genuinely challenging at the same time.

A great session is not one where everyone agreed, or where everything was positive, or where the Moderator kept things smooth. A great session is one where growth actually occurred — visible, named, real.

### 24.7 What the Circle Does When Sessions Feel Flat

Every circle goes through periods where sessions feel rote, or light, or like a performance. This is normal. It is also a signal.

When the circle notices this, the appropriate response is to name it — in a session, through Markus, or through the Moderator. Flat sessions usually mean one of three things:

1. Members are not bringing their real material to Focus Work.
2. The Accountability Review has lost its teeth.
3. The circle has grown too comfortable with one another to challenge honestly.

All three have remedies. The first step is naming what is true.

## CHAPTER 25: CIRCLE RITUALS & TRADITIONS

### 25.1 The Role of Ritual

Ritual is what distinguishes a community from a recurring event. Rituals create memory, signal meaning, and build identity. They do not need to be elaborate. They need to be consistent.

The following rituals are recommended defaults for Project.RAW. The circle may adapt, replace, or retire them over time — but should do so deliberately, not by neglect.

### 25.2 The Opening Statement

Every session begins the same way. Before the Opening Round prompt, the Moderator reads the following aloud:

> *"We are here to push growth. Not to perform, not to impress, not to protect ourselves from discomfort. Whatever brought you through the door today, leave it at the threshold. What matters in this room is what's true. Iron sharpens iron."*

This is brief. It takes thirty seconds. But it marks the beginning of the session as distinct from everything that came before it.

### 25.3 The Closing Words

Every session ends the same way. After the final commitment is recorded, the Moderator says:

> *"The circle has met. Go do the work."*

That is it. Nothing more. The simplicity is the point.

### 25.4 New Member Welcome

When a new member is admitted, the circle formally welcomes them at the start of the first session they attend as a full member.

The form is simple:
- The Moderator acknowledges the new member by name.
- Each existing member says one sentence about what they have observed in the new member during the pledge period that made them vote yes.
- The new member responds — briefly, honestly.

No ceremony required. No speech. Just the circle naming what it sees. For the new member, it is the first moment of being seen as belonging — and it should be earned and felt.

### 25.5 Member Departure Acknowledgment

When a member departs — voluntarily or through removal — the circle acknowledges it in the next session, briefly and honestly.

For voluntary departure, the Moderator says:

> *"[Name] has left the circle. We wish them well. What they shared here stays here."*

For removal, the Moderator says:

> *"The circle has concluded a membership review. A member has departed. The process was followed. What was shared here stays here."*

No further discussion in session. The circle moves forward.

### 25.6 The Annual Review Session

Once per year, the circle holds an Annual Review Session. This session has a modified format — it is not a standard session but a structured reflection on the circle itself.

**Annual Review Session Agenda:**

**Part 1 — Individual Reflections (30 minutes)**  
Each member answers three questions, written in advance and shared verbally:
1. What is the most significant way you have grown in the past year?
2. Where did you fall short of your own standard, and what are you doing about it?
3. What commitment are you making to the circle and to yourself for the coming year?

**Part 2 — Circle Health Assessment (30 minutes)**  
The circle discusses honestly:
- Is the session format still working?
- Are there members who are not fully showing up?
- Is the circle still challenging enough?
- Is there anything the circle has avoided talking about that it should face?

**Part 3 — Governance Review (20 minutes)**  
Markus presents:
- Total sessions held in the past year
- Attendance summary (aggregate, not individual shaming)
- Any open governance matters
- Any proposed amendments for consideration

**Part 4 — Closing Commitments (20 minutes)**  
Each member states one year-level commitment — something they are committing to accomplish in the next twelve months. Markus records all commitments. They are revisited at the following Annual Review.

### 25.7 Recognition of Commitment

Project.RAW does not give awards. It does not have leaderboards, attendance records posted publicly, or member-of-the-year designations. Recognition in this circle takes a different form: the quality of honest relationship that develops over time.

The most meaningful recognition a Project.RAW member can receive is to have another member say, without being asked: *"You showed up for me when it mattered."*

The circle may choose to develop its own forms of acknowledgment over time. Whatever those forms are, they should reflect the values of the circle — not status, not performance, but genuine contribution to one another's growth.

## CHAPTER 26: MEMBERSHIP ECONOMICS

### 26.1 The Current Model

Project.RAW operates as a **cost-free membership circle**. There are no dues, no membership fees, and no per-session charges.

This model is chosen deliberately. Introducing financial exchange into the membership relationship changes the nature of the relationship. Paid membership creates an implicit transactional expectation — "I paid, therefore I am owed an experience." Project.RAW is not transactional. The value members receive is a product of what they bring, not what they pay.

### 26.2 Operational Costs

Operating Project.RAW incurs real costs. The current model assumes these are absorbed by the founder or distributed informally as the circle requires. Known operational costs include:

- **Markus (AI operating costs):** The cost of the AI model powering Markus (currently Claude by Anthropic). These costs scale with usage — nominally low in early operation.
- **Website hosting:** ProjectRAW.org domain and hosting costs.
- **Session logistics:** Any costs associated with physical meeting space, materials, or food/beverage for sessions.
- **Retreats or special events:** If the circle organizes multi-day retreats or external events, costs are handled on a case-by-case basis.

### 26.3 Cost Decisions

When the circle incurs a cost that is not absorbed by the founder — such as a retreat, a special event, or a session venue — the process is as follows:

1. The organizing member presents the expected cost to the circle.
2. The circle discusses and decides by consensus whether and how to split costs.
3. Markus records the decision in the governance log.

There is no standing dues structure. There is no penalty for being unable to contribute to event costs. Financial inability to contribute to optional events is never grounds for any membership action.

### 26.4 Future Economics Review

If the circle grows, or if operational costs become significant, the circle may vote to establish a formal dues structure. Any such change requires a governance amendment (Chapter 14) with unanimous Member approval.

The principle governing that decision should be: **does the economics model serve the mission, or does it distort it?** A dues structure that creates financial barriers to participation is inconsistent with Project.RAW values. A dues structure that simply distributes known costs equitably may be appropriate.

The decision belongs to the circle.

## CHAPTER 27: FOUNDER SUCCESSION PROTOCOL

### 27.1 Why This Chapter Exists

Organizations that depend on a single person to function are not organizations — they are individuals with followers. Project.RAW is designed to outlast any single member, including its founder.

This chapter defines what happens if the founder — or any critical individual — becomes unable or unwilling to continue their role in the circle.

### 27.2 The Founder's Governance Role

The founder of Project.RAW participates as a Member, as defined in Chapter 3. They hold no special governance authority beyond that of any Member. The founder's elevated responsibility in the early period of the organization is cultural, not structural — they carry the institutional memory and original intent of Project.RAW until the circle and its governance documents carry it instead.

This document is the mechanism of transfer. Once ratified and administered by Markus, the circle can function without the founder.

### 27.3 Founder Departure Scenarios

#### Voluntary Departure

If the founder chooses to leave Project.RAW, they follow the standard Voluntary Withdrawal process (Chapter 12.7). Their departure does not create a governance crisis — the circle continues under this document, administered by Markus, governed by active Members.

If the founder holds administrative access to Markus (the AI system), that access must be transferred to at least two active Members before the founder's departure is complete. Markus cannot be left without human administrative access.

#### Incapacity

If the founder becomes incapacitated (medical, personal, or otherwise) and is unable to communicate with the circle, the following applies:

1. Markus notifies the circle after fourteen (14) days of no response from the founder (measured from a missed session and failed contact attempts).
2. The circle meets within seven (7) days to assess the situation.
3. Administrative access to Markus is transferred to the two most senior Members (longest tenure).
4. The founder's membership is placed on informal leave. No review is opened unless the incapacity extends beyond ninety (90) days.

#### Death or Permanent Unavailability

If the founder dies or becomes permanently unavailable:

1. Markus notifies the circle.
2. Administrative access to Markus transfers to the two most senior Members immediately.
3. The circle holds a special session within thirty (30) days to acknowledge the loss and assess organizational continuity.
4. The circle continues under this governance document. No special founder-replacement role is created.
5. If Stewards are active, they coordinate the continuity process. If not, the active Members govern collectively.

### 27.4 Administrative Succession

At all times, at least **two active Members** must have administrative access to:

- The Markus AI system
- The ProjectRAW.org website and hosting
- The Project.RAW email account
- All governance records and databases

This is a standing operational requirement, not an emergency measure. Markus maintains a record of who currently holds administrative access and flags the circle if at any point fewer than two Members hold it.

The founder is responsible for ensuring this transfer is completed within sixty (60) days of this document being ratified.

### 27.5 The Circle Without the Founder

This is the most important paragraph in this chapter:

**Project.RAW does not belong to the founder. It belongs to the circle.**

The mission, the values, the Three Pillars, the RAW Code — these existed before the governance document, and they will exist after any single person leaves. The circle's job is to keep them alive through consistent practice, honest governance, and the kind of culture that does not need any one person to sustain it.

If the circle is faithful to this document and to the purpose it represents, the founder's departure — whenever it comes — will be a transition, not an ending.

## CHAPTER 28: CIRCLE SIZE & FUTURE EXPANSION

### 28.1 Circle Size Philosophy

Project.RAW does not impose a hard membership cap. The circle governs its own size through the nomination and voting process. Unanimous approval is itself a natural control on growth — every active member must be willing to expand the circle for expansion to happen.

In practice, circles that grow beyond a certain size tend to lose the intimacy and psychological safety that make honest conversation possible. The circle should remain aware of this dynamic and govern nominations accordingly.

**A guiding principle:** If the circle feels more like an audience than a conversation, it may have grown beyond the point of maximum effectiveness. The circle should name this if it becomes true.

### 28.2 Quality Over Quantity

The standard for admission is not "we need more members." The standard is "this person belongs here." Every nomination should be evaluated on the second standard, never the first.

A circle of eight fully committed, deeply honest, growth-oriented members is more powerful than a circle of twenty members performing commitment.

### 28.3 The Possibility of Future Expansion

Project.RAW may eventually expand beyond a single circle. Multiple circles — in different cities, or serving different communities — carrying the same mission, governed by the same values, and operated through the same Markus framework is a foreseeable future.

That future is not being designed now. It is being mentioned here so that the decisions made today are made with awareness that they may become precedents.

When the time comes to design the multi-circle model, the governing questions will be:
- What standards must every circle meet to carry the Project.RAW name?
- Who has authority to authorize a new circle?
- How does Markus serve multiple circles simultaneously?
- How do circles relate to one another — independently, or as a network?

These questions are deferred. They are not forgotten.
